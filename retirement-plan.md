# Retirement Plan: 56 → 60 (Australia)

A four-year plan to retire at 60, built around your super, your share portfolio, and the sale or wind-down of the florist business.

> **Important:** This is general information and planning, not personal financial advice. Tax and super rules change every 1 July, and Centrelink thresholds change every March and September. Figures below were current as of the 2025–26 year; check them on ato.gov.au and servicesaustralia.gov.au before acting. Before selling the business or making large super contributions, see a licensed financial adviser and an accountant who knows the small business CGT concessions. The money involved justifies the fee.

## 2. The target

### How much will you spend?

The ASFA Retirement Standard puts a "comfortable" retirement for a homeowning couple at about **$76,000 a year** (2025). That covers private health insurance, a reasonable car, regular dining out, and a domestic trip each year, with occasional overseas travel.

This plan assumes you want more than that:

- **$95,000 a year** (in today's dollars) as the long-term budget
- **Plus $15,000 a year for the first 10 years** (ages 60–69) for travel and the "active years"

Track your actual spending for the next 3 months. It's the single most useful number in this plan, and most people guess it wrong.

### Key ages

| Age | What happens |
|---|---|
| 56 (now) | Four years of maximum saving and business preparation |
| 60 | **Preservation age.** Once you retire, you can access all your super tax-free |
| 60 | Start account-based pensions; sell or close the business |
| 65 | Super fully accessible even if you're still working; minimum drawdown rises from 4% to 5% |
| 67 | **Age Pension age.** You likely won't qualify at first (too many assets), but a part pension usually starts later as balances fall |

## 3a. Part-time work, long service leave and inheritance

These are the three biggest levers besides the business sale. All of them are optional; the base case above assumes none of them.

### Part-time work from 60 to 64

You don't have to stop work completely to access your super. **After 60, ending any employment arrangement counts as a condition of release**, even if you start a new part-time job afterwards. The super you had when that job ended becomes fully accessible. So you can sell or step away from the florist business, start your super pensions, and still work part-time. That could include working a few days for the new owner as part of the handover.

Part-time work helps in three ways:
1. Every dollar earned is a dollar you don't take out of super while markets are still compounding it.
2. Your employer pays super guarantee (12%) on the wages, and you can add salary-sacrifice contributions within the $30k cap.
3. It eases the shift from running a business to full retirement, which many small business owners find hard.

**Tax:** your super pension income is tax-free after 60 and doesn't count towards your tax bracket. Only the wages are taxed. Each person has an $18,200 tax-free threshold, so modest part-time pay attracts little tax.

**Alternative if you keep working longer hours:** a *transition to retirement* pension lets you draw 4–10% of your super a year while still working. It's less tax-effective than a full retirement pension, because earnings in a transition to retirement pension are taxed at 15%.

### Long service leave

- If one of you is an employee, long service leave is paid out on termination and taxed as ordinary income (with concessional rates for any leave accrued before August 1993).
- **Timing matters.** Taking the leave *before* you resign, as paid leave, spreads the income and keeps you on the payroll (with super guarantee paid on it). A lump sum all in one year can push you into a higher tax bracket. Check your award or state long service leave act: rules differ between states.
- Self-employed florists don't accrue long service leave unless they're employed by their own company under an award. Your accountant can confirm whether this applies.
- In the calculator, enter the **after-tax** amount and the age it's paid. It's added to your shares portfolio. You could instead contribute part of it to super as a personal deductible contribution, if you have cap room, and reduce the tax on it.

### Inheritance

- There's no inheritance tax in Australia. However, if you inherit **assets** (shares or property other than the deceased's home), their capital gains carry over to you, and you pay CGT when you sell them.
- Inherited **super** paid to an adult child usually has up to 17% tax deducted from the taxable component.
- If you're on the Age Pension when it arrives, the inheritance counts under the assets and income tests straight away. You must tell Centrelink within 14 days.
- Up to $120,000 per person a year (or $360,000 using the bring-forward rule) can go into super as non-concessional contributions, where earnings in pension phase are tax-free.
- **Don't count on it.** Inheritances often arrive later, or are smaller, than expected because of aged care costs. Make sure the plan works without it, and treat it as a buffer or an estate top-up.

## 4. The four-year action plan

### Year 1 (age 56–57): set up and get the facts

- [ ] **Value the business.** Get an informal valuation from a business broker who sells florists. Small retail florists usually sell for a multiple of adjusted owner earnings plus stock and fit-out. A sound lease matters a lot to the value.
- [ ] **Confirm how long you've owned the business.** If it's 15 years or more by sale, the **15-year exemption** may make the entire capital gain tax-free (see section 5).
- [ ] **Track 3 months of household spending** to set your real retirement budget.
- [ ] **Max out concessional contributions:** a $30,000 cap per person in 2025–26 (check the 2026–27 figure). This includes employer contributions. If you're self-employed, make personal deductible contributions and lodge a *notice of intent to claim* with your fund before you lodge your tax return.
- [ ] **Check carry-forward contributions.** If either of you had a **total super balance under $500,000 on 30 June** of the prior year, you can use unused concessional cap from the past 5 years. Check each person's balance separately. The lower-balance partner may qualify.
- [ ] **Review super investments and fees.** Compare your fund's fees and its option (balanced, growth or high growth) against the market. A 0.5% fee difference on $1.1m is about $5,500 a year.
- [ ] **Review insurance inside super.** Life and TPD cover may no longer be worth the premiums, since you have assets and no mortgage. Don't cancel income protection while you still depend on business income.

### Year 2 (age 57–58): make the business saleable

- [ ] **Reduce how much the business depends on you.** Train a senior florist to run the shop day to day. Buyers pay more for a business that runs without the owner.
- [ ] **Clean up the financials.** Two to three years of clear profit-and-loss statements, with personal expenses separated out.
- [ ] **Secure the lease.** Negotiate an option or a lease term that will transfer to a buyer. A short or uncertain lease kills sale prices.
- [ ] **Document key relationships:** wedding and event clients, wholesalers, corporate accounts, website and online orders.
- [ ] **Keep maximising concessional contributions.** Consider the **spouse contribution offset** if one partner earns under $40,000.

### Year 3 (age 58–59): tax strategy and the sale

- [ ] **See an accountant about the small business CGT concessions before you list the business.** The sale structure (selling shares in a company versus selling the business's assets) changes the tax outcome.
- [ ] **Decide where the proceeds go.** Under the CGT concessions, eligible proceeds can go into super via the **CGT cap** (about $1.78m in 2025–26) *without* using your non-concessional cap. Strict deadlines apply, and you must lodge an election form when you contribute.
- [ ] **Plan non-concessional contributions.** $120,000 a year per person, or $360,000 over three years using the bring-forward rule if your total super balance is low enough. Shares can be sold down gradually over two tax years to manage capital gains, then contributed.
- [ ] **List the business** about 12–18 months before your target date. Florist sales often take 6–12 months.

### Year 4 (age 59–60): transition

- [ ] **Decide on part-time work** from 60–64 (for example, a handover role with the business buyer) and on when to take any long service leave.
- [ ] **Build a cash buffer:** 12–24 months of spending ($100k–$200k) in a high-interest account or the cash option in your super. It means you never have to sell shares in a downturn.
- [ ] **Settle the sale.** Consider staying on for a short handover period, and agree it in the contract.
- [ ] **At 60, after you stop work:** advise your fund you've met a condition of release, then start an **account-based pension** each. Earnings in pension phase are tax-free, and withdrawals after 60 are tax-free.
- [ ] **Keep balances within the transfer balance cap** ($2m per person from 1 July 2025). Any excess stays in accumulation.
- [ ] **Book your private health insurance and Medicare review** before employer or business cover changes.

## 5. The business sale: small business CGT concessions

These can be worth hundreds of thousands of dollars. You need a specialist accountant, but here are the basics:

**Basic eligibility:** the business has turnover under $2m, *or* your net assets (excluding your home and super) are under $6m. The business must also be an "active asset."

| Concession | What it does | Relevance to you |
|---|---|---|
| **15-year exemption** | The entire capital gain is tax-free if you've owned the business 15+ years and you're 55+ and retiring | **Best outcome.** Proceeds can go to super via the CGT cap |
| **50% active asset reduction** | Halves the gain, on top of the general 50% CGT discount | Applies if you don't qualify for the 15-year exemption |
| **Retirement exemption** | Up to $500,000 of gain tax-free (lifetime limit per person). Because you're over 55, you don't have to contribute it to super | Can be stacked with the active asset reduction |
| **Rollover** | Defers the gain if you buy a replacement asset | Not relevant if you're retiring |

## 6. Retirement income strategy (from 60)

**Order of drawdown:**
1. **Account-based pensions** for regular income. Take at least the minimum: 4% under 65, 5% from 65–74, 6% from 75–79, then rising.
2. **Shares** to top up when needed. For retirees on low taxable incomes, dividends are often taxed little or nothing, and franking credits are refunded.
3. **Cash buffer** to cover spending during market falls, refilled in good years.

**Investment mix:** a balanced-to-growth mix (around 60–70% growth assets) is typical for a 30+ year retirement. Going too conservative at 60 is a common mistake, because inflation is the bigger risk over three decades.

**Tax:** in retirement your super income is tax-free, and each person has an $18,200 tax-free threshold (higher in practice with the low income tax offset, and higher again with the seniors offset (SAPTO) from 67). Hold the shares in the name of whichever partner has less other income.

## 7. Age Pension and concessions (from 67)

- **Assets test (homeowner couple, Sept 2025):** you get the full pension below about $481,500 of assessable assets, and it cuts out at about $1.07m. Your home isn't counted. Super in pension phase *is* counted.
- On the base case you'll be over the limit at 67, but a **part pension usually starts in your early 70s** and grows to the full rate by your mid-80s.
- **Commonwealth Seniors Health Card:** income-tested only (super pensions are deemed). You'll probably qualify at 67 even without the Age Pension. It gives you cheaper PBS medicines and other concessions. Apply at 67.
- **Gifting limits:** $10,000 a year, and no more than $30,000 over five years, per couple. Gifts above this still count as your assets for 5 years. Keep this in mind before helping children with large amounts.

## 8. Estate and protection

- [ ] **Wills:** update both, especially once the business is sold.
- [ ] **Binding death benefit nominations** on both super accounts. Without one, the trustee decides who gets your super. Check whether yours lapse every 3 years.
- [ ] **Death benefits tax:** super paid to adult, non-dependent children can be taxed at up to 17% on the "taxable component." A **re-contribution strategy** (withdraw tax-free after 60, then re-contribute as non-concessional) can reduce this. Discuss it with your adviser.
- [ ] **Enduring power of attorney and advance care directive** for both of you.
- [ ] **Downsizer option (later):** if you sell your home after 55 (owned 10+ years), you can each put up to $300,000 into super outside the normal caps. This is useful if you downsize in your 70s.

## 9. Risks and what to watch

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| A market crash near 60 (sequence risk) | Cash buffer of 1–2 years; don't sell growth assets in a downturn |
| Business sells for less, or doesn't sell | The base case already assumes $0, so you can still retire |
| Living to 95+ | Age Pension safety net; consider a lifetime annuity for part of super |
| Overspending in the early years | Review spending yearly; the "go-go" budget is time-limited |
| Health costs | Keep private health insurance; budget for rising premiums |
| Rule changes (e.g. the proposed extra tax on super balances over $3m) | Your balances are well below $3m; revisit each July |
