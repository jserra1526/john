What the model decides

Every month, milk collected in each region must go somewhere: to a plant, onto the spot market (up to a limit), or, as a last resort, dumped at a heavy penalty. Milk cannot be stored between months.

At each plant the milk is treated as its components, fat and protein. Each product consumes a fixed amount of each component per tonne, including process losses. Butter takes almost only fat, skim milk powder takes protein, and cheese and whole milk powder take both. Fat or protein that no product uses is sold as a low-value by-product.

Products go into stock and are sold against each month's demand. Sales cannot exceed the market maximum. Missing a contract minimum costs a shortfall penalty.

Limits it respects

  • Plant intake: tonnes of milk a plant can receive each month.
  • Line hours: each line has hours per month and a rate in tonnes per hour for each product it can make. Products on one line share its hours.
  • Storage: maximum tonnes of each product in stock.
  • Shelf life: stock held must be sellable within its shelf life (zero means no stock, as for fresh cream).
  • Closing stock: minimum stock of each product at the end of the horizon.

Reading the shadow prices

The solver also reports what each limit is worth. A line cell showing $6,110/hr means one more hour on that line in that month would add about $6,110 of margin. A milk value of −$200/t means an extra tonne of milk in that month loses $200 after paying for it, because the plants are already full.
